# Tail status for the corrected level-2 form factor

## Outcome C

The corrected finite coefficient system is exact. A bandwidth-one infinite
form factor does not currently follow from the available argument.

Bian's Theorem 1 has the logical form

\[
F_\kappa(\alpha,T)
=\text{spike}+P_{\kappa,B}(\alpha)+o_{\kappa,B}(1),
\qquad 0<|\alpha|<1,
\]

for each fixed truncation parameter `B`. The error constant depends on `B`.
The proof supplies no summable majorant for the corrected coefficients and no
uniform remainder after `B` tends to infinity. It therefore permits recovery
of every fixed finite coefficient, but it does not permit either of the
following limit exchanges:

\[
B\to\infty\quad\text{inside}\quad T\to\infty,
\]

or

\[
|\alpha|\to1^-\quad\text{after an uncontrolled infinite sum}.
\]

The geometric remainder in thesis Lemma 1 is not enough by itself. That bound
holds for the approximate Dirichlet series in the half-plane
`Re(s)>=1+epsilon`. Downstream contour and mean-square estimates introduce
`B`-dependent constants, including the displayed

\[
O_{\kappa,B}\left(x(\log x)^{2\kappa B+3}\right).
\]

No bound in the thesis controls the complete chain uniformly in `B`.

## Sharpest present statement

Assuming the hypotheses used by the pair-correlation argument, for every fixed
integer `J` the regular part has the corrected finite expansion

\[
F_2(\alpha,T)
=\sum_{i=1}^{J} C_{2,i}|\alpha|^i
+\mathcal R_{J}(\alpha,T),
\]

where each fixed coefficient is generated by `exact_c2.py`. The existing proof
only gives `R_J=o_J(1)` in its fixed-truncation regime. It does not give a
numerical one-sided bound for `R_J` over bandwidth one.

Consequently:

- the corrected polynomial is a finite asymptotic expansion, not a complete
  pointwise formula;
- the direct recurrence and completed-CUE calculations remain falsification
  oracles, not tail bounds;
- no window constant or simplicity percentage may be promoted from a finite
  truncation.

## Exact sufficient condition for a window

Let

\[
A_v(u)=\int v(s)v(s-u)\,ds
\]

be the autocorrelation of an admissible window. If a future argument supplies
a uniform remainder bound

\[
|\mathcal R_J(\alpha)|\leq\varepsilon_J
\quad\text{for all }|\alpha|\leq1,
\]

then its error in the bilinear window functional is at most

\[
\varepsilon_J\int|A_v(u)|\,du
\leq\varepsilon_J\left(\int|v(s)|\,ds\right)^2.
\]

This bound applies to the full window space. For a finite-dimensional window
class `V`, a sufficient uniform requirement is therefore

\[
\varepsilon_J
<\frac{\text{desired strict margin}}
{\displaystyle\sup_{v\in V}\|v\|_1^2}.
\]

The numerator and denominator must both be enclosed before optimization can
produce a theorem-level number. Neither is presently available for the
corrected level-2 input, so the window phase remains locked.

## Missing mathematical input

Any one of the following would remove the barrier:

1. a closed form for the corrected `F_2` on `|alpha|<=1`;
2. an absolutely convergent corrected coefficient series with an explicit
   uniform coefficient majorant;
3. a direct bound on the weighted remainder operator over the full admissible
   window space;
4. a version of the explicit-formula and mean-square argument whose constants
   are explicit and uniform in the truncation level.

Until one of these exists, the corrected coefficients stop at an exact formal
system plus fixed-order asymptotics. That is the quantified obstruction, not a
positive percentage.
