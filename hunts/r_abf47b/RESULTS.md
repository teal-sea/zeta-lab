## 2. Redundancy of the 57 Pair Orbits
The inherited first step from R-31B6C1 proposed scaling the sparse algebraic sieve using the 57 exact $H \times S_2$ pair-orbit skeletons. However, since $S_8$ acts transitively on perfect matchings, every triple of monochromatic matchings $(M_a, M_b, M_c)$ can be mapped by $S_8$ to one starting with $M_0$. The subgroup of $S_8 \times S_3$ preserving $M_0$ (allowing color swaps of the remaining two) is exactly $H \times S_2$. Thus, the 57 pair orbits merely refine the 31 triple orbits. They add no new branches that the 31 do not already cover. Scaling the sieve to 57 skeletons is therefore redundant.

### A. The Groebner step does not test Support 7

`run_groebner.py` builds Hunt #73's Support-7 system with `build_system` from
`hunts/r_044dd2/polynomial_sieve.py`, then hands `sympy.groebner` the three
monochromatic target brackets as generators, next to the 6,558 relations. Rebuilt on
landing: the targets have 15, 15 and 20 terms, all of degree 4; the relations are
homogeneous of degree 3 (1,458 of them) or 4 (5,100); no generator has a constant
term; 138 variables appear. An ideal generated by homogeneous polynomials without
constant terms lies inside the maximal ideal of the origin. The origin is a common
zero, so no Groebner basis of this ideal is `[1]`, and a finished run could not have
excluded Support 7 with any solver.

Two things are wrong with the encoding. On a witness the target brackets are nonzero,
so entering them as zero generators asks a different question, one the origin always
answers. And no saturation by the variables is imposed, although Support 7 is a
statement about the torus on which all 138 supported variables are nonzero, and the
relations had their common torus factors removed on that understanding. The 300 s
timeout therefore says nothing about Support 7. The closing sentence of section 3 does
not follow either: a compiled backend would compute a basis of the wrong ideal faster.

The question Hunt #73 asks (`hunts/r_044dd2/RESULTS.md`, Result 4) is whether the
product of the three target brackets lies in the relation ideal saturated by the
variables. Its fixed-degree spans screen for that membership at bounded degree: cap 3
and cap 4 found none, and cap 5 stopped at a measured fill-in wall. A Groebner test that
can decide infeasibility asks whether `<relations, 1 - y*T0*T1*T2*x_0*...*x_137>` is the
unit ideal, which holds exactly when no point of the torus satisfies every relation
with all three targets nonzero.

`test_orbit_refinement.py`, added on landing, pins the generator census above. It also
runs a two-variable control: the relation `x + y` with the target `x + y` is
infeasible, yet the encoding this run used returns the proper basis `[x + y]`, while
the saturated encoding returns `[1]`.

### D. What stands: the refinement in section 2

Section 2 is right. Inside S8 x S3, the subgroup that maps the slice {M0} x PM x PM to
itself is H x S2, so two slice points in one H x S2 orbit lie in one S8 x S3 orbit. S8
is transitive on the 105 perfect matchings, so every S8 x S3 orbit meets the slice.
The 57 pair orbits therefore map onto the 31 triple orbits: 8 triple orbits contain one
pair orbit, 20 contain two and 3 contain three. Two pair-orbit skeletons over one triple
orbit are S8 x S3 images of each other, so sieving both repeats the work of one branch.
Hunt #74's handback thread, to scale the sieve to the 57 pair-orbit skeletons, covers
nothing the 31 branches do not.

Two routes agree. `map_orbits.py` enumerates all 1,157,625 triples, in about 130 s of
CPU at landing. Its first printed line writes 57 as a literal; the mapping it reports
is computed. `test_orbit_refinement.py` stays on the 11,025-point slice. It checks that
the seven generators give the whole 384-element stabilizer of M0, recovers 57 and 31
and the fibre sizes, and checks each of Hunt #71's 31 orbit sizes as 105 times that
orbit's slice count. Grade: exact finite check, two implementations. It is an
elementary orbit-stabilizer argument and no novelty is claimed.
