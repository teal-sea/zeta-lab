# S4 over F_13[T]: the biquadratic theta has no scalar local identity

**Hunt #148, step 3. Verdict: no.** In F_13[T] the coefficients of the biquadratic (n = 4)
theta function at squarefree indices are computable, and they do not vanish identically
beyond small degree; but at three primes their modulus depends on the primes themselves, not
on their degrees, so no choice of the component at infinity and no normalization by degree
makes them multiplicative up to phases. A probe built on them with quartic averaging
therefore has no scalar local Euler identity: its principal row is not
zeta(4z) L(w) / L(x, eta) times an Euler product. The first failing term is the coefficient
of X^3 in the principal row. Exact computation at the stated parameters, not reviewed by any
person; files produced on branch `hunt/ff-probe`, not landed. Every number here is read from
`results_step3.json` and pinned by `test_ffprobe.py`.

## 1. The coefficients, and the checks at n = 4

Over F_q(T) with q = 1 mod n, the theta function's Fourier coefficients at an index r are
Patterson's rho_0(r, eps, i), one for each of the n components i of the infinite place,
computed from the sums C(r, eps, i') of n-th order Gauss sums over the monic moduli of
degree i' (`SOURCES.md`, formulas 5 to 7). Here q = 13, n = 4, the quartic symbol is
(a/c)_4 = chi(Res(c, a)), and eps sends g^3 to i for g = 2, the least primitive root. Since
13 = 5 mod 8, eps(chi(-1)) = -1, so the two additive characters of `SOURCES.md` give
different signs, and both are checked.

- rho_0(1, eps, i) = 1, 13 tau(eps chi), 0, 0 for i = 0, 1, 2, 3, and at a linear prime P,
  rho_0(P, eps, i) = 1, 0, 13^2 tau(eps chi^2), 0: Patterson's values for n = 4 (2007,
  page 252), with his c_o = (q-1)/n dropped.
- The finite Hecke relation holds in all 36 checked cases, the Hecke relation at infinity in
  all 20 checked cases in each additive character (with the signs of `SOURCES.md`), and
  rho_0(r b^4) = rho_0(r) at the checked r.
- Patterson's four relations for n = 4 (2007, page 252): C(r, eps, 2) = tau(eps) q
  C(r, eps, 1) eps(chi(-1)) and C(r, eps, 3) = tau(eps^3) q^3 at degree 2, and
  C(r, eps, 3) = tau(eps^2) q^2 C(r, eps, 1) at degree 3, hold in the 2014 character at every
  checked r (3, 3 and 2 of them). The degree-4 relation, C(r, eps, 3) = tau(eps) q^2
  C(r, eps, 2), fails at r = (T-1)(T-2)(T-3)(T-4); the relation that the Hecke relation at
  infinity gives there, C(r, eps, 3) = tau(eps) q C(r, eps, 2), holds (a misprint or an
  extraction error, q^2 for q).

## 2. Components and forced zeros

For a squarefree index r of degree d and a component i: whether rho_0 vanishes at every
sampled r, whether it must (R < 0, or i = i' in the Hecke relation at infinity), and the
moduli of the values at the sampled r (`component_table`; at degree 3 the samples are
(T-1)(T-2)(T-3), (T-1)(T-2)(T-5) and (T-1)(T^2+2)).

| 0 | 1 | 46.87 = 13^1.5 | 0 (R < 0) | 0 (R < 0) |
| 1 | 1 | 0 (i = i') | 609.34 = 13^2.5 | 0 (R < 0) |
| 2 | 1 | 13 | 609.34 | 7921.40 = 13^3.5 |

| 4 | 0.5547, 0 | 26, 0 | 338, 0 | 15842.79, 0 |

**Every component family vanishes on an odd degree class.** A family that reads the
coefficient at r in the component i(r) = (b - deg r) mod 4 must vanish, by R < 0 or by
i = i', at every degree d = 2b + 1 (mod 4) (derived from the Hecke relation at infinity,
checked in the table and by `test_ffprobe.py` through degree 11). For n = 3 the same
computation leaves two families (b = 0, 1) with no forced zero and kills the third, and for
n = 2 it recovers the classical theta's support on even degrees. With b = 1, for instance,
rho_0 = 1 at every linear prime and rho_0 = 0 at every squarefree index of degree 3, which no
function multiplicative up to phases can do.

## 3. The degree-3 coefficients depend on the primes, not on their degrees

Read the coefficients by degree, with any component choice. At degree 0, 1 and 2 the
squared moduli are fixed by the component alone: at every linear prime they are 1, 0, 13^5,
0, and at every pair of linear primes 1, 13^2, 13^5, 13^7, for i = 0, 1, 2, 3
(`moduli_by_lambda`, twelve primes and 21 pairs; the irreducible quadratic of the table
agrees). At degree 3 only i = 1 and i = 3 survive, and there rho_0 is a constant times
Patterson's C(r, eps, 1) = tau(eps chi) H(r), with

    H(r) = sum over a in F_13 with r(a) != 0 of conj(eps(chi(r(a)))),

the classical character sum of a cubic polynomial (with this hunt's additive character
e = e_o(a_-1); Patterson 2007, page 253, prints -tau(eps) for the 2007 character). Over all
2028 squarefree monic cubics (`degree3_census`), the squared modulus abs(H(r))^2 is:

| factorization of r | abs(H)^2 = 0 | nonzero | nonzero value |
|---|---|---|---|
| three linear primes (286) | 234 | 52 | 52 |
| linear times irreducible quadratic (1014) | 546 | 468 | 26 |
| irreducible cubic (728) | 0 | 728 | 13 |

For r = (T-1)(T-2)(T-lambda), lambda = 3, ..., 12, H(r) vanishes except at lambda = 5 and 11,
where abs(H)^2 = 52. A function whose modulus is multiplicative over primes, times any factor
depending on the degree or even on the factorization type, is constant in modulus on each
row of this table. These coefficients are not: on three linear primes the modulus is 0 for
234 triples and 26 for 52 (at i = 1). The ratio of section 4's notation,
abs(f(P1 P2 P3))^2 abs(f(P1) f(P2) f(P3))^2 / (abs(f(P1 P2) f(P1 P3) f(P2 P3))^2 abs(f(1))^2),
is defined only at lambda = 5 and 11 for the admissible component choices
(`three_body`), and the zeros at the other eight values of lambda are the defect. This is
the hypergeometric behaviour Patterson describes for three prime factors (2014, lines
470 to 481).

## 4. Consequence for the probe, and the named term

In the F_13[T] analogue of the probe, with quartic symbols, quartic averaging and the theta
coefficients read by degree, the principal row's coefficient at an index A with s = a = 1 is
K(A) = F(1, A, 1) theta(A) eta(A) times phases of modulus one, and
F(1, A, 1) = g_{xi chi_A}(T A, 1) is the Gauss sum of a primitive character modulo T A for
squarefree A prime to T, of squared modulus 13^(1 + deg A) (the q = 7 analogue is checked in
`results_ledger.json`). So abs(K(A)) is, up to the degree, abs(theta(A)), and section 3 is a
statement about K. A scalar local identity would make K multiplicative up to phases, and it
fails first **at the coefficient of X^3 in the principal row, at A = (T-1)(T-2)(T-lambda)**,
which vanishes for eight of the ten lambda and not for the other two. The squarefree
coefficients do not vanish identically beyond small degree (section 2), so the verdict is
not the S3 one (a theta supported on squares); it is that the coefficients exist and are not
multiplicative.

## 5. What the template would price, and what that is worth

Hunt #136's order-k template (`PROBE.md` there, sections 3 and 4, derived and measured
there, unreviewed) prices k = 4 at 7/8 for the balanced probe, 13/16 at the per-summand
corner and 4/5 at perfect row counts (recomputed here as exact fractions,
`results_ledger.json`), and at 0.8105686 for the endpoint with the paper's row counts carried
over (hunt #136's variant A, measured there, quoted and not rerun). All four are conditional
on hunt #136's L1 to L3 (closed-form squarefree coefficients with a pairing, a twisted
reflection, a scalar Euler identity). In F_13[T], L1 fails in the sense of sections 2 and 3,
so the template prices nothing for this probe. Over F_q[T] the truth is 1/2 for every
L-function concerned (Weil).

## 6. What transfers to Q(i), and what does not (inferred)

- The forced zeros of section 2 come from the infinite place of F_q(T), which is
  non-archimedean with n components; the infinite place of Q(i) is complex and has no such
  components. That obstruction does not transfer.
- The degree-3 behaviour of section 3 is a statement about the finite places: the
  coefficient at three primes is not determined by the coefficients at fewer primes. Over
  number fields the Hecke theory of Kazhdan and Patterson likewise stops determining the
  squarefree coefficients of degree-n theta functions for n >= 4 (Deligne's non-uniqueness
  of Whittaker models, as hunt #136 records via David, Dunn, Hamieh and Lin). The
  function-field computation is evidence, not proof, that S4's L1 fails over Q(i) for the
  same reason.

## 7. Limitations

- One q (13) and one embedding eps; components chosen per degree (a rule choosing a
  component per index would not be the theta function's coefficients in any natural sense);
  degree-4 data only as samples.
- The verdict is about scalar Euler identities of Lemma 7.1's type. A probe whose principal
  row is not an Euler product could still isolate 1/L by another mechanism; none is proposed
  here.
- The quartic probe itself (its Poisson coefficients and local factors at q = 13) is not
  built: the obstruction is located in its theta input, and the reduction from K to theta in
  section 4 uses only the Gauss-sum modulus of a primitive character.
