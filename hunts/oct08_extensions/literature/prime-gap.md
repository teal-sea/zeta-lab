# Bounded literature check: seventh-power intervals

Checked 2026-10-08. This is a prior-art assessment, not a proof audit or a priority certificate. No producer proof or computation was changed.

## Exact comparison target

Assume every nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta-function has real part at most 7/8, equivalently there are no such zeros in the open half-plane Re(s)>7/8. For **every integer n>=1**, prove a prime exists **strictly** between n^7 and (n+1)^7. Boundary zeros with real part exactly 7/8 are permitted. Full RH is a stronger hypothesis; eventual existence, finite verification, and almost-prime existence are different conclusions.

**Assessment: adjacent prior results authenticated; priority unresolved.** This bounded search did not authenticate an earlier statement with this exact hypothesis and all-n conclusion. That absence is not evidence of priority. The useful distinction in the present artifact is explicit coverage of every n under the stated 7/8 assumption, not discovering eventual seventh-power intervals or the use of zero-density estimates.

The elementary implication from eventual cubes to eventual seventh powers does not require a new analytic theorem: for sufficiently large integer n, set m=ceil(n^(7/3)). Then m^3>=n^7 and m+1<n^(7/3)+2<(n+1)^(7/3), so the cube interval (m^3,(m+1)^3) lies in the seventh-power interval. The finite range left by such enormous cube thresholds is precisely why an all-n result is materially different.

The current multistrip construction uses published density constants rather than claiming new zero-density theory. See also [Kadiri–Lumley–Ng, primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.12263), Lemma 4.14, equation (4.71), and Table 1. Their exact applicability and the explicit-formula source were checked separately in [the source audit](../audits/prime-gap/SOURCE-CHECK.md) and [the seventh-power audit](../audits/prime-gap-multistrip/AUDIT.md).

## Search scope and safe wording

Searches included combinations of “seventh powers”, “primes between”, “7/8”, “seven-eighths”, “quasi-Riemann hypothesis”, “short intervals”, and “consecutive powers”; follow-up searches used author names and exact titles. Sources searched were the general web index, arXiv full texts, publisher full texts, and the reference chains of the papers above. Several general queries returned poorly matched results. This was not a systematic MathSciNet/zbMATH search, exhaustive forward-citation analysis, or a search of every recent OpenAI-consequence manuscript. Some current arXiv endpoints are mutable; explicit versions are supplied where verified.

Suggested description: “Assuming that zeta has no nontrivial zeros with real part greater than 7/8, we give an explicit proof, with independently replayed interval arithmetic, of primes between consecutive seventh powers for every positive integer. This improves this project's earlier ninth-power consequence. Related all-n results under full RH and unconditional eventual results for smaller powers are classical or already published; priority for this exact weaker-hypothesis statement is not established here.”

Do not describe the result as unconditional, the first all-n seventh-power theorem, a new asymptotic prime-gap exponent, or a new general density method. The local written proof and arithmetic audit are distinct from Lean formalization.
